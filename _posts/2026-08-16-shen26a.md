abstract: Sampling representative nodes from large graphs is fundamental to graph
  signal processing and network analysis, yet existing methods require access to the
  full graph Laplacian, making them impractical at scale. We propose a simple and
  effective column-selective graph sampling algorithm based on a minimum inner product
  greedy selection rule. At each iteration, the algorithm accesses only a small random
  subset of Laplacian columns, requiring no eigendecomposition or global graph traversal,
  making it well-suited for large-scale graphs where the full Laplacian cannot be
  stored in memory. We analyze the algorithm under the stochastic block model and
  show that, when the degree distribution is balanced across nodes, the algorithm
  achieves sampling proportional to cluster size, and that the resulting mean estimate
  is controlled for band-limited graph signals in the Paley-Wiener space, with the
  error decaying as inter-cluster connectivity weakens. Numerical experiments on both
  synthetic and real-world data validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
